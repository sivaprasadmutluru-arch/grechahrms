CARR Implementation Plan

How to add Clarity, Accountability, Results and Rewards to the existing HRMS without duplicating employee, organization or performance data.

Prepared: August 24, 2026Audience: Product owners, HR leaders and engineeringStatus: Recommended architecture

Executive Summary

CARR means Clarity · Accountability · Results · Rewards. It is a structured agreement for each role or employee: why the role exists, what it owns, how success is measured and how strong results are recognized.
Grecha HRMS can support it without a second employee system. Reuse current employees, departments, job roles, reporting managers, performance cycles, objectives/key results, feedback, bonus points, roles and tenant schemas.
The main missing piece is a versioned CARR record and workflow. New entities are needed for CARR templates, responsibilities, ownership, KPI targets, approval stages, reviews, rewards, history and dashboard aggregates.
Implement it in controlled releases. Start with role library, employee CARR, responsibilities, KPIs and approval/publishing. Add reviews and rewards next; add AI generation only after enough approved CARR data exists.

What CARR does in practical HR terms

C · Clarity

Define the role

Purpose, job description, scope, reporting line, expected behaviours, authority and boundaries. An employee should understand what the company expects and what is outside the role.

A · Accountability

Name the owner

Every responsibility has one accountable owner, collaborators, evidence requirements and review frequency. This prevents duplicate ownership and responsibilities with nobody accountable.

R · Results

Measure outcomes

Weighted KPIs and OKRs define target, actual value, unit, period, data source and status. Results should measure outcomes—not only tasks completed.

R · Rewards

Recognize achievement

Review scores can feed bonus points, recognition, variable-pay recommendations or development actions. Rewards remain governed and approved, never automatically paid from an unreviewed score.

Core distinction: a job description describes a position; a CARR makes that position operational and measurable for a specific employee and period.

Recommended product structure

1. Role Library

Reusable role templates containing purpose, job description, competencies, default responsibilities, KPIs and review cadence.

2. Employee CARR

A period-specific copy assigned to one employee. Managers tailor targets without changing the master role template.

3. Responsibility Register

Accountable owner, collaborators, evidence, frequency, priority and overlap warnings.

4. KPI / OKR

Target, actual, unit, weight, direction, due date, source, confidence and RAG status.

5. Approvals and Reviews

Draft → manager → department head → HR → approved → published, followed by periodic check-ins.

6. Rewards Framework

Recognition or bonus recommendation based on approved results, with caps and HR/payroll approval.

Workflow that should be implemented

Role template→Assign employee→Set responsibilities & KPIs→Submit→Manager / HR approvals→Publish→Review→Reward decision

Recommended states

DRAFT, SUBMITTED, MANAGER_REVIEW, DEPT_HEAD_REVIEW, HR_REVIEW, APPROVED, PUBLISHED, RETURNED, ARCHIVED.

Every transition must record actor, timestamp, comment and before/after version. Published CARRs are immutable; edits create a new version.

Scoring and dashboard definitions

0–100

CARR Health Score

Recommended initial formula: Clarity 25%, Accountability 25%, Results 35%, Rewards/Review readiness 15%. Make weights company-configurable.

Avoid a misleading score

Health is a governance-quality score, not the employee’s performance rating. Keep the performance result separate from document completeness and approval timeliness.

Phased implementation

Phase 0 — Product rules and prototype

Confirm terminology, approval chain, score definitions, reward policy, eligible employees and review cadence. Produce clickable screens and API contracts.

Phase 1 — Minimum usable CARR

Role templates, employee CARR, responsibilities, weighted KPIs, draft/submit/approve/publish, permissions, audit events, Excel import/export and a basic coverage dashboard.

Phase 2 — Reviews and rewards

Self/manager reviews, evidence, calibration, reminders, overdue queues, reward rules and approved bonus-point integration.

Phase 3 — Analytics and integrations

Department adoption, KPI risk, responsibility gaps/overlaps, scheduled reports, notification automation and payroll reward handoff.

Phase 4 — AI assistant

Generate draft CARR from a job description, suggest measurable KPIs, detect duplication and summarize risks. AI output must remain a suggestion requiring human approval.

Recommended next steps

  1. Approve the business definition: CARR is a versioned employee/role agreement, not another employee profile.
  2. Select one pilot organization, 2–3 departments and one review cycle.
  3. Finalize the approval chain and identify who may publish or alter reward rules.
  4. Build Phase 1 behind a tenant feature flag so current HRMS behavior remains untouched.
  5. Migrate no legacy performance data automatically; link existing objectives only after mapping rules are validated.
  6. Measure pilot coverage, approval time, overdue reviews, responsibility gaps and user adoption before enabling rewards or AI.

Further questions to decide before coding

  • Is one CARR created per employee, per job role, or both? Recommendation: template per role, versioned instance per employee and cycle.
  • Who is the final publisher: HR, department head or both?
  • Can employees edit KPI actuals, or must values come from managers/integrations?
  • Are rewards bonus points only, monetary recommendations, recognition, or all three?
  • Should branch managers see only their branch or the full company hierarchy?

Caveats and assumptions

  • The screenshot is treated as a product concept, not evidence of an existing CARR industry standard or finished Grecha module.
  • No production database counts were queried; therefore adoption values shown in the reference screenshot are not used in this report.
  • Existing PMS entities provide useful foundations, but schema and authorization tests are required before linking production objectives to CARR KPIs.
  • AI generation should not be part of the first production release because approved examples, privacy rules and evaluation criteria are not yet established.
